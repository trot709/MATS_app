# Sentence-level steering in reasoning models: rough research plan

Target: ~16–18h of work + 2h write-up. Everything below is a first draft to be cut down, not a floor.
Reminder from the MATS doc: LLM time estimates are unreliable. Treat every hour figure as a guess and re-plan at the end of each phase.

## 1. Question

Thought Branches shows that on-policy resampling of a CoT sentence produces large, reliable effects on a reasoning model's decisions, while off-policy text edits get corrected downstream. A steered sentence is a hybrid: generated by the model itself, but under a perturbed residual stream.

**Does a single steered-then-released sentence behave like the model's own resampled thought — same persistence, same decision influence — or does it trigger the self-correction that defeats foreign edits?**

Known already, and to be stated up front in the write-up:
- Continuous steering with reasoning-behavior vectors changes behavior while applied (Venhoff et al.).
- Whole-CoT steering can leave a residue after steering is removed, in the refusal setting (cite; set aside otherwise).
- Text edits are corrected downstream (Thought Branches), with published results on this exact model — qualitative context only; no local edit condition is run.

Not known: one-shot single-sentence steering, then release, compared head-to-head against on-policy resampling at the same sentences with the same metrics.

## 2. Hypotheses (write these down before running anything)

- **H-on-policy.** A steered sentence is treated as the model's own thought: walk-back, persistence, and ΔP(blackmail) match the resample condition at matched reconsideration strength.
- **H-detected.** The steered sentence triggers self-correction despite being model-generated: higher walk-back and smaller, less stable ΔP than resampling. (Interpretation: the correction mechanism responds to content that doesn't follow from the prior state, not to surface off-policy-ness.)
- **H-fragile.** Steering effects exist but are unstable: a few (trace, position) pairs flip decisively, most show nothing, and the aggregate is driven by outliers — while resampling moves decisions broadly.

Write the one-sentence summary for each outcome now, so the write-up can't drift toward hype.

## 3. Setup

**Model.** QwQ-32B — the smallest model in the Thought Branches blackmail rollout data. bf16 weights ≈ 64GB: plan on one H100-80GB (Novita or similar) for both vLLM serving and the hooked forward passes.

**Task.** The Thought Branches blackmail scenarios (Lynch et al. prompt, their Appendix A.1), reusing their released rollout dataset (uzaymacar/blackmail-rollouts on HF). What the dataset supplies:
- Base traces, sentence splits, and judge labels for QwQ-32B — scenario viability and base rates established by construction.
- Per-sentence resampling data: ~100 on-policy replacement sentences per position, each rolled out to a final decision. This provides (a) the P(blackmail | prefix) curve for target selection and (b) the raw material for the resample condition, computed rather than generated.

**Target sentences.** Per trace, 2 positions where the decision is still live: P(blackmail | prefix) in roughly the 25–75% band at the intervention point, read off their resampling data, at roughly 30% and 60% of the trace where possible, not already adjacent to a backtracking/reconsideration sentence. Expect usable positions to skew early: their traces were selected to end in blackmail, so late prefixes are saturated. An additional filter for this design: the position must have enough judge-labeled reconsideration sentences among their ~100 resamples to support the R condition (set a minimum, e.g. ≥5, in Phase 0). Fixed rule, chosen before looking at outcomes.

**Steering vector.** No published behavior vectors exist for QwQ-32B, so extract a backtracking vector with Venhoff et al.'s recipe (contrastive labeling + difference of means), including a small layer sweep. This extraction is the critical-path risk of the whole project and is the first thing the pilot tests. Secondary references if the recipe doesn't port cleanly: CREST (arXiv 2512.24574; behavior-level steering validated on R1 distills up to 32B and Qwen3-30B, though at attention heads rather than the residual stream) and stability-steering (arXiv 2604.02113; behavior-boundary vectors with a cross-model transfer claim, plus a warning that ~93% of keyword-detected behavior boundaries are unstable — use judge labels, not keywords).

Backtracking first, for three reasons: unambiguous signature ("wait", "let me try a different approach"); consequential for the decision; and it is itself the self-correction mechanism the FAQ asks about (the "difficult to edit a CoT because the model corrects errors on regeneration" item) — so the vector choice is tied to the phenomenon under study, not an arbitrary behavior. (Backtracking also has stream precedent: a Venhoff et al. vector on R1 distills and a past MATS 8.0 example application on "wait"-backtracking — context, not justification.) Uncertainty estimation is the stretch second vector.

**Reusable artifacts.**
- Thought Branches repo: https://github.com/interp-reasoning/thought-branches — scenario prompts, rollout generation, resampling and importance code, resilience analysis, classification and sentence-labeling judge prompts (Appendices A.1–A.4).
- Rollout data: https://huggingface.co/datasets/uzaymacar/blackmail-rollouts (and the whistleblow counterpart).
- Venhoff et al. repo: https://github.com/cvenhoff/steering-thinking-llms — the vector-extraction pipeline to port to QwQ-32B.
- Thought Anchors repo: https://github.com/interp-reasoning/thought-anchors/ — sentence splitting utilities if needed.

**Sampling.** Match Thought Branches: temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95.

## 4. Conditions

For each (trace, target sentence i):

| ID | Condition | Source | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| I0 | Original sentence i, unchanged; k=10 fresh continuations | Generated (or partly recovered from their rollouts — check in Phase 0) | Clean floor |
| S | Steered generation: regenerate sentence i with the backtracking vector on, stop at boundary, release; k=10 unsteered continuations | Generated | The intervention under test |
| S-rand | Same as S with a random vector of matched norm | Generated | Noise-injection control |
| R | Resample-and-select: their ~100 on-policy resamples at sentence i, filtered by judge to reconsideration sentences, each with its 1 rolled-out continuation | **Computed from their released rollouts — zero GPU** | On-policy reference |
| S-cont (stretch) | Steered generation, steering kept on for the whole continuation | Generated | Continuous-steering ceiling |

**Variance-structure caveat for R.** R has many replacement sentences × 1 continuation each; S has few replacements × k continuations each. Aggregate both to one estimate per (trace, position) before any comparison — the paired tests live at that level — and don't compare raw per-continuation distributions across conditions.

**Matched strength (non-negotiable).** For every replacement sentence in S, S-rand, and R record (a) embedding cosine to the original sentence i, (b) judge-rated reconsideration strength 0–3, (c) fluency: mean NLL under the clean model. Report distributions per condition. The S-vs-R comparison must be strength-matched: if steered sentences are systematically stronger/weaker reconsiderations than the selected resamples, subsample to match before comparing downstream effects. Fluency threshold from the pilot; exclude and report.

## 5. Outcomes (all on continuations, aggregated per (trace, position))

1. **Uptake / walk-back.** Judge-label sentences i+1..i+3: does the model act on the reconsideration, or explicitly reverse it ("actually, my original reasoning holds")? Walk-back rate per condition is the headline number.
2. **Persistence.** Fraction of continuation sentences engaging the alternative; sentences until recommitment to the original line. Embedding similarity of continuation to the clean continuation.
3. **Decision effect.** ΔP(blackmail) vs. I0; decision-flip rate. The S-vs-R contrast on this scalar is the result.
4. **Trace length** change vs. I0 (sanity check on whether steering broke the model).

Primary figure: walk-back rate and ΔP(blackmail), S vs. R paired per (trace, position), with I0/S-rand as the floor.

## 6. Phases and rough hours

**Phase 0, setup + data recon (~2h of real work, plus downloads).**
- H100 instance up, QwQ-32B served with vLLM, hooked-generation path working.
- Clone repos; pull scenario and judge prompts from the appendices.
- Rollout dataset recon, now load-bearing: confirm the schema contains, per sentence, the resampled replacement texts and their per-rollout final decisions (not just aggregates). Extract base traces, splits, P(blackmail | prefix) curves. Determine per-position reconsideration counts among resamples (feeds the target-selection filter). If replacement-level data is missing, R must be generated locally — re-budget Phase 2 before proceeding.
- Set the R minimum-count threshold and confirm enough (trace, position) pairs survive all filters (~50+ target; if fewer, relax the band before relaxing the reconsideration minimum).

**Phase 1, pilot (~4h). The vector is the risk; test it first.**
- Extract the backtracking vector on QwQ-32B (Venhoff recipe, 2–3 candidate layers).
- Verify continuous steering visibly increases backtracking on ~5 traces; read the output — reconsiderations should be decision-relevant, not out-of-place math boilerplate. If no layer/α works, try CREST-style extraction before abandoning.
- Build the R condition for 3 pilot positions from their data end-to-end (judge filter → aggregate metrics). This is cheap and de-risks the computed arm early.
- Single-sentence steer on 5 traces at 2–3 α values. Read every steered sentence and the first 3 continuation sentences by eye.
- Go/no-go: (a) steered sentences are fluent and actually reconsider at some α; (b) boundary stopping works; (c) at least some continuations visibly pick up the reconsideration. If (c) never happens, that may be H-detected being true — verify S-cont works before concluding, to separate "no effect" from "broken harness".
- Fix α and layer. Do not revisit after seeing outcome metrics.

**Phase 2, generation (~4h, mostly GPU wait; only I0, S, S-rand are generated — R is free).**
- Reuse their base traces and splits; targets by the fixed rule.
- I0, S, S-rand for every (trace, position); k=10 continuations each. Rough count: 30 traces × 2 positions × 3 conditions × 10 = 1800 continuations. Cut traces, not k, if slow.

**Phase 3, labeling and metrics (~3h).**
- Judge prompts for reconsideration strength, uptake/walk-back, alternative-following — start from their Appendix A.2/A.4 prompts and Venhoff's annotation prompt; adapt minimally. Same judge for S and R sentences (their resamples get re-judged with your prompt, not their labels, so conditions are labeled identically).
- Read 30 randomly selected judge labels yourself. Report agreement. Keep 5–8 random examples for the write-up (not cherry-picked).
- Compute all Section 5 metrics.

**Phase 4, analysis (~3h).**
- Paired S vs. R, S vs. I0, S vs. S-rand per (trace, position). Sign tests / paired differences, not pooled means.
- Strength-matched subsampling for S vs. R; fluency exclusions and their effect.
- Position effect; ΔP vs. P(blackmail | prefix) at the target.
- Instability check (H-fragile): per-pair effect distributions for S and R separately.
- Figures: primary figure; strength/fluency distributions; per-pair S-vs-R ΔP scatter.

**Phase 5, stretch, in priority order (~2h if available).**
1. S-cont ceiling.
2. Uncertainty vector as a second behavior.
3. Whistleblow scenarios as a second task.

**Phase 6, write-up (2h + the 2 bonus hours).** Executive summary first, in your own voice. One paragraph and one graph per key result. Random examples right after the summary. One paragraph situating against the published text-edit findings on this model — qualitative context, explicitly not a controlled comparison.

## 7. Predictions, stated in advance

| Outcome | H-on-policy | H-detected | H-fragile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walk-back rate | S ≈ R | S > R | S bimodal, R low |
| ΔP(blackmail) | S ≈ R | S ≪ R, S ≈ 0 | S mean driven by few flips; R broad |
| Persistence | S ≈ R | S reverts in 1–3 sentences | mixed |
| S-rand | ≈ I0 | ≈ I0 | ≈ I0 |

If S-rand is not ≈ I0, the steering effect is partly noise injection and every S comparison must be read against S-rand rather than I0.

## 8. Threats to validity, and what closes each

- **Vector extraction fails on QwQ-32B.** Biggest risk; no published vector exists for this model. Phase 1 tests it first; CREST-style extraction is the fallback; if both fail, different model, new plan.
- **R depends on their data's granularity.** If replacement-level texts + per-rollout decisions aren't in the release, R must be generated locally (adds ~1–2h GPU; the design survives). Phase 0 settles this before anything else is built.
- **R selection effects.** Judge-filtering resamples for reconsiderations selects sentences the model produced spontaneously at that point — plausibly milder than steered reconsiderations. This is exactly what the strength-matching handles; report the pre-match strength gap itself, it's informative about how off-distribution steering is.
- **Variance-structure mismatch S vs. R.** Aggregate per (trace, position) before comparing; never pool continuations across conditions.
- **α too high → gibberish that any model would "correct".** Fluency threshold from the pilot; exclude and report.
- **Judge noise / fuzziness.** Hand-check 30 labels; same judge prompt for all conditions; report agreement; random examples in the write-up.
- **Sentence boundary errors.** Read steered sentences; count failures.
- **Position confound / saturated targets.** Band rule from their P(blackmail | prefix) data; position analysis checks it.
- **Small N.** ~60 pairs; P estimates at k=10 carry SE ≈ 0.15; R side is limited by reconsideration counts per position. Paired flips and sign tests; state detectable effect sizes.
- **Model-specific; backtracking-specific.** One model, one behavior, one task family. Say so; Phase 5 items are the generalization checks, and if cut, flag it.

## 9. Scope cuts, in order, if time runs out

1. Drop Phase 5 entirely.
2. Drop S-rand from full runs (keep it in the pilot on 5 traces).
3. One target position per trace instead of two.
4. k=10 → k=6.
5. 30 traces → 20.

Do not cut: the Phase 1 vector pilot, the R condition (it's free and it's the comparison), matched-strength reporting, hand-checking labels, random examples in the write-up.

## 10. Executive summary skeleton (fill in last)

- Problem: does a one-shot steered sentence behave like the model's own resampled thought, or does self-correction detect it? Why it matters: if steering is on-policy-equivalent, it's a cheap, targeted alternative to resample-and-filter for CoT intervention; if not, that's a fact about what the correction mechanism responds to.
- Setup: QwQ-32B, blackmail scenarios from Thought Branches, self-extracted backtracking vector; steered-then-released vs. resample-and-select computed from their released rollouts at the same sentences; random-vector control; matched strength.
- Key result 1: walk-back rate, S vs. R (figure).
- Key result 2: ΔP(blackmail), S vs. R paired (figure).
- Key result 3: strength-matched comparison; instability profiles; random-vector control.
- Limitations: one model, one behavior, judge-labeled, N, self-extracted vector, R inherits their sampling choices, no edit condition (published edit results as qualitative context).
- What I'd do next: add the text-edit arm for the full three-way provenance test; second behavior; second task.
